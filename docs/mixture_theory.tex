\title{The mixture form of the Version-4 basket model}
\author{Ajit}

\begin{abstract}
We replace the Version-4 basket law by its mixture form: each trip draws a taste
$z\sim\N(0,I)$ of dimension 16, then a basket from the existing exact law given $z$. In the current
form a taste is weighted by the prior times $Z_z$, the total weight of all baskets that taste allows.
On the Dunnhumby refit this weighting hid a region of 120-product baskets that held most of the
probability. Training never visited it and validation mostly missed it: the reported held-out
log-likelihood was $-38.8$ per trip, the value found when the region is included about $-53.8$. In the mixture
form a taste is weighted by the prior alone. Each basket's posterior over tastes is then strongly
log-concave, baskets are drawn exactly, and the likelihood estimate is a lower bound with bounded
weights. On 64 held-out real trips the mixture form scores $-35.98$ against $-36.78$ for the old fit;
on synthetic data $-27.38$ against $-27.90$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The current form and why it hides modes}

A basket $S$ has $n_c$ products in category $c$ and $m$ categories present. The current law is
\begin{equation}
p(S)=\frac{e^{E(S)}}{Z},\qquad E(S)=E_0(S)+\tfrac12\lVert s(S)\rVert^2,
\end{equation}
where $E_0$ holds the product utilities, within-category terms, size term and competition shift, and
$s(S)=\sum_{j\in S}\phi_j-\sum_c (n_c-1)_+\psi_c$ is the basket's taste vector (rank 16). The Gaussian
identity $e^{\frac12\lVert s\rVert^2}=\int\N(z;0,I)e^{z\cdot s}\,dz$ gives
\begin{equation}
p(S)=\int \underbrace{\frac{\N(z;0,I)\,Z_z}{Z}}_{w(z)}\;\underbrace{\frac{e^{E_z(S)}}{Z_z}}_{p(S\mid z)}\,dz,
\qquad E_z(S)=E_0(S)+z\cdot s(S).
\end{equation}
Given $z$ the law $p(S\mid z)$ is exact: $Z_z$ comes from the category-by-size dynamic program and
baskets are drawn by backward sampling. The difficulty is the weight $w(z)$.

\paragraph{The tilt.} $w(z)$ is the prior multiplied by $Z_z$. A taste that makes many large baskets
attractive has an astronomically large $Z_z$. On the refit, regions where 120-product baskets dominate
had $\log w$ about 50 to 90 nats above the region near the data.

\paragraph{Several peaks.} $\log w(z)=\log\N(z;0,I)+\log Z_z$ is concave plus convex, so it can have
several local maxima. A fixed-point search found a giant-basket peak on 63 of 64 held-out trips.

\paragraph{Training cannot see them.} The likelihood gradient is
\begin{equation}
\nabla\log p(S)=\nabla E(S)-\E_{w(z)}\big[\nabla\log Z_z\big].
\end{equation}
Stochastic maximum likelihood estimated the second term with persistent Gibbs chains started at the
observed baskets, about four sweeps per epoch. They never reached the far peak, so its weight was never
pushed down.

\paragraph{Validation cannot see them.} The held-out estimate of $\log Z$ built its proposal from chains
annealed from $\beta=0.25$. The far chains began in the giant region and left it during annealing. A
missed region makes $\hat Z$ too small and the reported log-likelihood too high.

\section{The mixture form}

A trip produces a basket in two steps: draw $z\sim\N(0,I)$, then draw $S$ from $p(S\mid z)$:
\begin{equation}
p(S)=\int\N(z;0,I)\,p(S\mid z)\,dz,\qquad p(S\mid z)=\frac{e^{E_z(S)}}{Z_z}.
\end{equation}
The law given $z$ is the one already implemented:
\begin{align}
E_z(S)&=\sum_{j\in S}u_j(z)-\sum_c\Big[\rho_c\tbinom{n_c}{2}+f_c(n_c)+(n_c-1)_+\big(z\cdot\psi_c-\tfrac12\lVert\psi_c\rVert^2\big)\Big]\nonumber\\
&\quad-\rho_0(m)+\sum_{c:\,n_c>0}\iota_c,\\
u_j(z)&=b_j-\tfrac12\lVert\phi_j\rVert^2+z\cdot\phi_j,
\end{align}
where $b_j$ carries price, display, mailer, discount, season, history and household taste, and $\iota_c$
is the within-category competition shift. For an observed basket,
$E_z(S)=E(S)-\tfrac12\lVert s(S)\rVert^2+z\cdot s(S)$.

The form is a mixture: one basket law per taste, weighted by how common that taste is. Products bought
together come from shared tastes. There is one component for every $z$, but all share one formula, so
the parameters do not grow with the number of components; the one choice is the dimension of $z$.

\begin{table}[htb]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lp{5.2cm}p{3.6cm}}\toprule
 & current form & mixture form\\\midrule
weight of taste $z$ & $\N(z;0,I)\,Z_z/Z$ & $\N(z;0,I)$\\
law given $z$ & $p(S\mid z)$, exact & the same\\
parameters & $b,\phi,\psi,\rho_c,f_c,\rho_0$, price, promotion, competition, history & the same\\
normaliser to estimate & $Z$, a 16-dimensional integral & none\\
drawing a basket & Gibbs chain & exact, two steps\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The forms share every parameter, so a current-form fit can start a mixture fit, but they are different
laws.

\section{Properties}

All four follow from one fact: $E_z(S)=a(S)+z\cdot s(S)$ with $a(S)$ free of $z$.

\begin{proposition}[convexity]
$\log Z_z$ is convex in $z$, with $\nabla_z\log Z_z=\E[s\mid z]$ and $\nabla_z^2\log Z_z=\Cov[s\mid z]\succeq0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\log Z_z=\log\sum_S e^{a(S)+z\cdot s(S)}$ is a log-sum-exp of affine functions of $z$; differentiate twice.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[one peak]
For an observed basket $S$, $\log p(z\mid S)$ is 1-strongly concave. It has a unique maximiser $m$,
which solves $m=s(S)-\E[s\mid m]$, and for every $z$
\begin{equation}
\log p(z\mid S)\le\log p(m\mid S)-\tfrac12\lVert z-m\rVert^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\log p(z\mid S)=-\tfrac12\lVert z\rVert^2+z\cdot s(S)-\log Z_z+\text{const}$, whose Hessian is
$-(I+\Cov[s\mid z])\preceq-I$ by Proposition 1. Strong concavity gives uniqueness and the quadratic
bound around the maximiser, where the gradient $s(S)-z-\E[s\mid z]$ vanishes.
\end{proof}
The current form's weight $\log w(z)$ is concave plus convex; the mixture posterior is concave plus
linear minus convex, which is concave.

\begin{proposition}[exact draws]
Drawing $z\sim\N(0,I)$ and then $S\sim p(S\mid z)$ by backward sampling gives an exact draw from $p(S)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[lower bound, bounded weights]
With $z_1,\dots,z_K$ drawn from a proposal $r$ covering the posterior,
\begin{gather}
\hat p(S)=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{\N(z_k;0,I)\,p(S\mid z_k)}{r(z_k)},\\
\E\,\hat p(S)=p(S),\qquad \E\log\hat p(S)\le\log p(S).
\end{gather}
If $r\ge\varepsilon\,\N(\cdot\,;m,I)$ with $m$ the maximiser of Proposition 2, then every weight is
bounded:
\[
\frac{\N(z;0,I)\,p(S\mid z)}{r(z)}\le\frac{(2\pi)^{d/2}\,p(S)\,p(m\mid S)}{\varepsilon},\qquad d=\dim z.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Unbiasedness is the importance identity; the inequality is Jensen's. For the bound,
$\N(z;0,I)p(S\mid z)=p(S)\,p(z\mid S)\le p(S)\,p(m\mid S)e^{-\frac12\lVert z-m\rVert^2}$ by Proposition 2,
and $r(z)\ge\varepsilon(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{-\frac12\lVert z-m\rVert^2}$.
\end{proof}
So the estimate can understate the fit but on average never overstate it, the opposite of the failure in
Section 1. A peak found only approximately loosens the bound but keeps the estimate valid.

\section{Training}

Training maximises the importance-sampled lower bound plus the production priors, with Adam, and every
$N$ steps applies moment matching and whitening. No Markov chain is used.

\paragraph{Objective and gradient.} For a batch of $B$ baskets and $K$ draws each ($K=8$ in the tests),
\begin{align}
\mathcal L&=\frac1B\sum_{t=1}^B\log\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{\N(z_{tk};0,I)\,p(S_t\mid z_{tk})}{r_t(z_{tk})}-\text{priors},\\
\nabla\mathcal L_t&=\sum_k\bar w_{tk}\big(\nabla E_{z_{tk}}(S_t)-\E_{S'\mid z_{tk}}[\nabla E_{z_{tk}}(S')]\big),
\end{align}
with normalised weights $\bar w$. The expectation is exact: it is the gradient of $\log Z_z$ through the
dynamic program.

\paragraph{Per batch.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find each basket's peak $m_t$ by gradient ascent on $\log p(z\mid S_t)$ with backtracking (8
  iterations on real data, 15 on synthetic).
\item Estimate $\Sigma_t=(I+\Cov[s\mid m_t])^{-1}$ with $\Cov$ from 32 exact draws of $S\mid m_t$.
\item Draw $K$ tastes from $r_t=0.8\,\N(m_t,1.2^2\Sigma_t)+0.2\,\N(m_t,I)$; the second part gives
  Proposition 4's bound.
\item Compute the $K$ terms $\log p(S_t\mid z_{tk})$ with gradients and take an Adam step.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Moment matching.} As in \texttt{scripts/fit\_interactions.py}: every $N$ steps the
product intercepts $\lambda$, the size term $\rho_0$ and the within-category terms $\rho_c$ each take one
damped step of their averaged gradient divided by the data rate (product purchase rate, frequency of each
category count, mean $\binom{n_c}{2}$), clamped at $\pm0.35$, $\pm0.5$, $\pm0.5$.

\paragraph{Whitening (parameter-expanded EM).} At a maximum of the likelihood the tastes pooled over
the baskets' posteriors have second moment $I$. Every $N$ steps we estimate
\begin{equation}
\Sigma=\frac1{N_b}\sum_t\sum_k\bar w_{tk}\,z_{tk}z_{tk}^\top,\qquad A=\Sigma^{1/2},
\end{equation}
over the $N_b$ baskets since the last step, and set $\phi_j\leftarrow A\phi_j$, $\psi_c\leftarrow A\psi_c$,
$\lambda_j\leftarrow\lambda_j+\tfrac12(\lVert A\phi_j\rVert^2-\lVert\phi_j\rVert^2)$. A model with prior $\N(0,\Sigma)$ equals
the rescaled model with prior $\N(0,I)$, and fitting $\Sigma$ to the pooled posterior is the EM update for
the prior's spread, so the step does not lower the likelihood. One term is not compensated: the
$\tfrac12\lVert\psi_c\rVert^2$ per extra product of a category becomes $\tfrac12\lVert A\psi_c\rVert^2$. Adam's state for
$\phi$ and $\psi$ is reset after each rescale.

\section{Generation and counterfactuals}

\paragraph{Generation.} Build $b_j$ from the trip's context, draw $z\sim\N(0,I)$, then $S\mid z$.
Repeating gives the trip's basket distribution; averages give incidence, size and category shares with
ordinary Monte Carlo error.

\paragraph{Counterfactuals.} Change the context (price, display, discount), which changes $b_j$ and
$\iota_c$, and redraw $S$ with the same $z$. Each draw is one shopper with a fixed taste seen before and
after the change, a paired comparison.

In the current form a price change also changes $Z_z$ and hence which tastes are common. In the mixture
form the taste mix is fixed by the prior and a price change only changes what each taste buys. This is a
modelling assumption suited to short-run price and promotion questions; effects on store traffic are
outside the model in both forms.

\paragraph{Recommendation.} Rank products for a trip by $P(j\in S)$, an average over $z$ of the exact
incidence given $z$. Conditioning on a partly observed basket $T$ uses $p(z\mid T\subseteq S)$. This is not
guaranteed log-concave: $\log P(T\subseteq S\mid z)$ is a difference of two convex functions. It needs a
peak check.

\section{Evidence}

All numbers are on the first 64 held-out trips of each world's validation split. The test weeks were not
opened.

\paragraph{Exactness.} In the test suite's tiny world (6 products, 3 categories, rank 2) and all 11 model
variants, $\log p(S\mid z)$ agrees with enumeration of every basket to $10^{-9}$, and the importance
estimate of $\log p(S)$ (4{,}000 draws) agrees with enumeration and $48\times48$ Gauss--Hermite
quadrature to $0.007$ nats, with effective sample size about $0.91$.

\paragraph{Cost.} One real step on 256 baskets takes 35 to 55\,s: about 1.6\,s per exact gradient call,
8 to 15 of them in the peak search and 8 in the objective. A damped Newton peak search was not faster
(25\,s against 30\,s) because its curvature came from 16 noisy draws.

\section{Limits and next steps}

\begin{itemize}
\item Shared tastes express products bought together, not cross-category substitution; within a
  category $\rho_c$ and the competition shift still do. No loss against the current model.
\item Generated baskets on real data were 28\% too small after 60 steps without corrections; the run
  with moment matching and whitening is the check.
\item The prior is one Gaussian. Real trip types may want a few Gaussian components; Proposition 2 holds
  within each.
\item Whitening leaves one term uncompensated, so it is not an exact EM step.
\item With few draws the training objective favours weaker tastes. On synthetic data $K=16$ reduced the
  excess spread at whitening (1.11 to 1.07) without changing the held-out result.
\item Cost: about 35--55\,s per real step against about 6\,s for the current step; a full epoch would take
  6--9 hours. Warm-starting each basket's peak applies from the second epoch.
\item Price and promotion checks must be re-run on the mixture form.
\end{itemize}
